## Scope and frame decisions

- Need counts use ids, not disposition verbs: 28 legacy ids remain, including ids retaining one part of a split; 19 new ids are SN-037–SN-055; 7 legacy ids are merged away (006, 014, 016, 017, 018, 020, 034); 028 is retired/reserved. Total authored needs: 47.
- All retained needs serve complete capture, trustworthy retained history, space efficiency, unattended operation or independent recovery. Notification, browsing, setup, usability and observability support those outcomes. No CI-proof, image-build, registry-publish or export-tooling need is introduced; these are test or delivery concerns under the fixed ruling. No delivery crossings are included.
- Lifecycle is explicit for setup (Provision) and per-launch capability, configuration, capacity and format checks (Startup); omitted lifecycle means Runtime. Container operation remains Runtime.
- The actual hats roster, not Appendix A’s older suggested tags, governs. Only store-mutation, store-format, filesystem, unattended, restore-host, scale and redistribution are used. Always-applicable integrity and security perspectives need no tag. No provision or ui tag is invented.
- Scope does not supply a license-cost ceiling, service-level deadline or numerical scale budget. SN-049 demands published operating-envelope budgets; their values remain a later SR/performance-budget decision requiring evidence, not invented numbers here. Third-party licensing questions reach relevant outcomes through redistribution and the LEGAL hat; no unsupported commercial licensing feature is added.
- The operational system includes backup, maintenance and both restore routes. Runtime, hashing, compression and optional media tools are external capabilities. Container startup/mounts are operation; image production and registry distribution are delivery and omitted. Source reads and writable working state are separate boundaries even when deployment places them on one device.
- EXT-16 means the complete handed-over backup pool, not a claim that each isolated snapshot subfolder contains all shared bytes. The saved-state selection stays independent of content location. Handoff completeness is explicit in DA-14.
- REL rows describe only external-to-external flows. Out-of-band actors have a relationship to the volume rather than a fictitious FileBackup mutation API. HomeHub appears only as an example in the generic backup-host entity.
- Appendix A’s blanket claim that particular filesystem families lack atomic rename is not adopted. DA-01 is conditional on the actual deployed access path; atomic visibility and crash durability are separate DA-01 and DA-16 claims. Lock release and common-lock-domain exclusion are likewise separate DA-02 and DA-03 claims.
- Integrity evidence is not authentication against a forged complete store (DA-11); a full digest is not a collision-proof identity (DA-05). Target name/time/property fidelity and host-tool availability are conditional, not blanket cross-platform promises.
- SUR-01 is evidenced only by the README local demonstration. DA-20 deliberately limits its fidelity to demonstrated local cases; no unobserved mocks, CI platform proof, network-filesystem simulation or universal stand-in is asserted.
